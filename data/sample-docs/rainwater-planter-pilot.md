# Rainwater Planter Pilot Field Notes

Harbor Hill is testing three small rainwater planters outside the fictional
Dockside community hall. The goal is to teach simple maintenance tracking, not
to model a real municipal water system.

Each planter has a label, a location, and one maintenance note:

- Planter R1 is beside the east gate. Its overflow tube was cleared on April 5.
- Planter R2 is near the bike racks. Its mulch layer was refreshed on April 12.
- Planter R3 is next to the notice board. Its valve should be inspected during
  the next Saturday session.

Volunteers record only non-sensitive observations: whether the soil is dry,
whether leaves are blocking the intake screen, and whether the planter label is
still readable. Volunteers do not record names, phone numbers, license plates,
or images of visitors.

The maintenance checklist has four steps:

1. Confirm the planter label matches the field note.
2. Clear loose leaves from the intake screen.
3. Check that the overflow path is not blocked.
4. Write one short note in the shared maintenance log.

If standing water remains for more than two days after rain, volunteers should
tell the Saturday shift lead. The shift lead decides whether to pause the pilot,
empty a planter, or ask the property manager for help.

The planter pilot is useful for RAG testing because it contains specific labels,
dates, and next actions. A grounded answer should be able to identify that R3,
the planter beside the notice board, needs a valve inspection.
